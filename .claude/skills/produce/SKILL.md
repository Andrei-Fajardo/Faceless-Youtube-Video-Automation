# /produce: one calendar item, from research to a finished video

Project: `<repo-root>` (git repo, studio in `aitrends-2d-studio/`). The plan lives in `calendar/calendar.json` (30 days, sized to the ElevenLabs free plan: one Short a day `D##-S1` and one long-form every Sunday `D##-L`, plus a backlog `B##` for later months and reserve topics `R##`). Days 1, 2, 3 and 7 are already made. Posting slots (Manila): Shorts 3:00 AM daily, long Sunday 11:00 PM. Status lives in `calendar/status.json`. Every topic has a unique `family` (the mechanism it explains); to change the plan edit `calendar/build_calendar.py` and re-run it, which refuses any repeat family or near-duplicate title (a research-failure swap must use a reserve topic, whose family is already checked). Helper: `python tools/produce.py show <day|next>`, `budget`, `item <id>`, `done <id> <path> <chars>`, `swap <id> <B##|R##>`, `status`.

## Arguments
- `status`: run `python tools/produce.py status` and `budget`, report, stop.
- `<day>` (number or date) with nothing else: produce the day's items that are not made, long first (Sundays only), then the Short `S1`. Stop after any item the budget cannot afford.
- `<day> long` or `<day> short 1`: only that item (a day has one Short, `S1`; a long exists only on Sundays: days 7, 14, 21, 28). `next`: the first item not made. `redo`: remake an item that is already made (new files; keep the old as `_v1`).
- Show the user which items you will make and the voice characters they need, then continue without asking (the user wants hands-off production). The only reasons to stop are in "Stop conditions".

## Hard rules
1. **Never publish or upload anything.** Output is a review packet in `finished/`.
2. **Facts only from sources.** Every spoken claim goes in `claims.md` with a source URL and `fetched` or `snippet`. Never invent a study, quote, date or number. If a key fact cannot be verified, rewrite more cautiously or `produce.py swap` the item for a reserve topic and say so.
3. **ElevenLabs guards are never raised or bypassed.** Check `python tools/produce.py budget` first. Caps: 1,600 characters per call, 2 paid calls per video, 30,000 characters a month (see `tools/eleven_config.json`). The voice is never EQ'd, compressed, limited or stretched; the mix script applies one fixed gain only.
4. **No ComfyUI, no image or video AI.** Everything is drawn by code in the studio, in the What If art style (stick figures, rim light, flat-shaded real objects).
5. Family-friendly. No gore, no swearing, no medical or legal advice, no tragedy as a joke. Items with a `risk` note get extra care (frame as a warning or as science, never as instruction).
6. Follow the quality, continuity and physics standards in `.claude/skills/what-if/SKILL.md` ("Quality standard for the drawn video" and "Continuity and physics standard"). Read those two sections once at the start of every invocation.

## Shell conventions
Start shell commands with: `cd "<repo-root>/aitrends-2d-studio" && export PATH="/c/Program Files/File Converter:/c/Program Files/Tracktion/Waveform 13:$PATH" PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 HF_HUB_DISABLE_SYMLINKS_WARNING=1 CHROMIUM_PATH="<path-to-chrome-headless-shell.exe>"`. Use system `python` and `node` (Node 26; `npx playwright install` does not work, the `CHROMIUM_PATH` override is how the renderer finds a browser). Write long files with the Write tool, never a shell heredoc. Wrap every pipeline command so it is timed: `python ../tools/audit.py run --video <slug> --step <step> -- <command>` (log steps you cannot wrap with `audit.py log`). Run renders longer than a minute in the background and keep working.

## Pipeline for one item
Slug = the calendar item's `slug`. Kind folders: What If items go to `finished/what-if/<slug>/`, Odd Detail items to `finished/odd-detail-js/<slug>/`.

**1. Preflight.** `produce.py item <id>`, `produce.py budget`, free disk over 10 GB (`df`). Estimate characters: long about 1,550, Short at most 650 (write Shorts tight; the plan leaves about 4,400 characters for retakes). If the budget cannot cover it, stop and report (do not touch the caps).

**2. Research.** At least three WebSearch queries for the idea's central facts (add the `rule`/hint from the calendar as the starting point), then read the key pages with the `web-fetch` agent for anything stated as a number, a name or a date. Prefer primary sources (papers, museums, standards bodies, company histories). Write `claims.md`. Note contested points and tell them as contested.

**3. Script** (`script.txt`, format `TOPIC: <title>` newline `SCRIPT: <one paragraph>`). Numbers in words except years; no digits, no quotation marks, no markdown, no em dashes; metric units.
- **What If Short:** 95-125 words (about 45-55 s), 600-750 characters. Arc: hook in action (no "what if" yet) -> the real human baseline with a number, then the extreme -> **ONE stated rule** that limits the power -> the title as a question -> three quick consequences (use "Plus:", "Minus:", "The catch?") -> the cost and a twist that uses the rule -> a callback line to the hook -> one-line call to comment. 4-6 ellipses before payoffs, 3-5 questions, average sentence 4-5 words, one dry joke. At least three sourced facts.
- **What If long (2:00-3:00):** 1,300-1,580 characters (one voice call, about 2:00-2:20; never exceed 1,600). Arc: cold open (the power in action, ending on a mundane detail) -> baseline, rule, title question -> consequence montage (upsides and downsides with real numbers) -> the hidden cost tied to a real mechanism -> the heart: one named relationship and a **planted detail** -> crisis and a stark either/or -> resolution (people over glory; pay off the planted detail) -> "Is any of this real?" (separate fact from fiction, name real studies only if verified) -> short call to action. Names: hero plus 1-3 side characters, short international names. At least five sourced facts.
- **Odd Detail Short:** 70-95 words. Hook of 8 words or fewer that opens a question about a daily object, word or symbol -> the problem -> who/how -> what -> the twist at about 70% -> a callback joke on the hook's word. Facts and dates only from sources; name the people; tell contested stories as contested.
- **Story logic gate (before voice is paid for):** write down the one rule, the cost, the planted detail and its payoff; read every line as a stranger; no two lines may contradict (numbers, abilities, choices); an ending that relies on a choice must have that choice established earlier. Count words, characters, ellipses, questions and digits with a small script. Save the check in `claims.md` under "Story logic".

**4. Voice.** `python ../tools/audit.py run --video <slug> --step voice --out ../finished/<kind>/<slug>/voice.mp3 -- python ../tools/eleven_whatif.py <slug> --base finished/<kind-folder>` (run from `aitrends-2d-studio`, or from the repo root without `../`). Check the take length: Short 35-58 s, long 1:55-3:00; if outside, fix the script and use the one remaining call.

**5. Project.** `python ../tools/produce_setup.py <slug> --aspect 16:9|9:16 --kind long|short --voice ../finished/<kind>/<slug>/voice.wav --script ../finished/<kind>/<slug>/script.txt --title "<title>"` (copies the template libraries: `_props.js` real-object props, `_world.js` sets, `_shotlib.js` helpers, `_kit.js` tags/keypads, `_devices.js` phone/calculator/desk phone/adding machine, `cast.js`). Then `python tools/transcribe.py --project projects/<slug> --script projects/<slug>/input/script.txt --lang en --model small` and `python tools/beats.py --project projects/<slug>`; print `timeline/sentences.json` times.

**6. Design the shots.** Short: 9:16, 20-25 shots (about one per 2-2.5 s). Long: 16:9, 40-50 shots (about one per 3 s). Every shot shows what the line says, when it says it. Rules learned the hard way:
- Draw people, props and anything in the camera's world INSIDE `layer(g, cam, 1, ...)`. Use `info.shot.start + t` for global time.
- Real scale: about 330 px per metre in rooms (figure `s = 2.4`), about 190 in yards (`s = 1.4-1.7`); desk 248 px high, piano keys 0.75 m, counter 298 px. Bodies that rotate are placed by their hip, standing bodies by their feet.
- The engine's brows are inverted for sad/worried/scared/cry and angry/determined: use `fixFace` from `_shotlib.js` (and pass `face: { brows: 'angry' }` for worried `bigFace` close-ups).
- Text: use `tag(g, text, x, y, { pill: true })` on bright backgrounds; 9:16 captions occupy y 1450-1590, keep graphics above y 1400; 16:9 keeps text inside the central 90%. No text over faces. One focal point per shot; fill every set with dressing (no bare walls).
- Recurring characters across 3 or more consecutive shots in one place = a **shared world state**: write `scenes/_<name>state.js` as a pure function of global time (hip position, mode `ground|air|water|carried`, kind), draw it from every shot, only the camera changes, and run `node ../tools/continuity_check.mjs <slug> <from> <to> _<name>state.js`. It must PASS before rendering (nobody on water, nobody floating, no jumps, full roster).
- Sound: write `story/shots.json` with a sound cue per beat from the library (`whoosh swish impact hit riser tick clock heartbeat pop blip counter ding shimmer stinger footstep buzz flash glitch powerup roomtone city hum wind murmur`), ambience per set, a music plan by section (moods: wonder, mystery, tension, calm, hopeful, sad). Cut on phrase boundaries; set `captions` true for Shorts, false for long; Shorts `bars: none`, long `bars: wide` for scenes and `none` for diagrams.
- Build the timeline with a small Python script (see the pattern in `aitrends-2d-studio/projects/half-brain` history) so shots are contiguous and `duration` = voice length + about 1.1 s.

**7. Review (two passes minimum).** `node render.mjs --project projects/<slug> --sheet --scale 0.5` and read the sheet(s); then `--stills <t1,t2,...>` at the riskiest moments (close-ups, anything physical, anything with text near the edges) and read them at full size. Fix and repeat until a pass finds nothing. Check: pictures match the words, no floating or overlapping objects, no clipped text, faces read as intended, consistent outfits and screen direction, a camera move in every shot.

**8. Render.** Short: `node render.mjs --project projects/<slug> --crf 16` (1080x1920). Long: `--crf 14` (2560x1440, style `duskhd`); run in the background. Then `python audio/mix.py --project projects/<slug>` (gain-only master, voice untouched). Measure with ffprobe/ffmpeg: duration, size, loudness (`ebur128`), silences over 1.2 s (must be 0), and build a strip: `ffmpeg -y -i <file> -vf "fps=1/N,scale=216:-1,tile=13x1" -frames:v 1 strip.png`. Limits: Short at most 60 s; long at most 3:00. Report loudness honestly (it will be below -14 LUFS because the voice is never limited).

**8b. Thumbnail (every item, Shorts too).** `python tools/thumb_setup.py <slug> --title "<title>" --folder <what-if|odd-detail-js>` creates `projects/<slug>-thumb` with the SAME aspect as the video (auto-detected): **Shorts get a 1080x1920 (9:16) thumbnail, long-form gets 1920x1080 (16:9)**. Never use a 16:9 thumbnail for a Short: YouTube centre-crops it on phones and cuts the words (user requirement, 2026-11-01, after "JUMP 10X HIGHER" showed as "UMP 10"). Replace `scenes/shots/t01.js` with the real design, then `python tools/thumb_render.py <slug> --folder <kind-folder>` writes `thumbnail_<W>x<H>.png` and a `.jpg` under 2 MB (YouTube's limit) into the packet. View it and iterate until it passes. Design rules: one idea and one focal subject (the video's central object or a big face, reuse the video's props and cast); at most 3 to 5 words of huge high-contrast text with a thick dark stroke, never repeating the title word for word if a shorter hook works; the video's own palette; it must still read at 320 px wide. **9:16 (Shorts):** stack the words on 2-3 lines, every word and the subject inside x 90-990 and y 150-1380 (the Shorts UI covers the bottom ~540 px and the right edge), background only below. **16:9 (long):** bottom-right 240x80 corner empty (duration badge), key content inside the central 1600x900. Example: `projects/what-if-you-could-jump-10x-higher-thumb/scenes/shots/t01.js` (9:16) and `projects/half-brain-never-slept-thumb/scenes/shots/t01.js` (16:9). To redo a thumbnail use `--force` (the old project is kept as `-old<N>`). Log the step with `tools/audit.py log`.

**9. Packet and bookkeeping.** In `finished/<kind>/<slug>/`: `final.mp4` (and `final_1440p.mp4` copy for long), `thumbnail_<W>x<H>.png` and `.jpg` (1080x1920 for Shorts, 1920x1080 for long), `contact_strip.png`, `script.txt`, `claims.md`, `post_copy.md` (3 title options, description with sources and the AI-disclosure line, hashtags, **a Tags section of at least 10 YouTube tags (comma-separated, the user requires this in every post_copy.md)**, TikTok and Instagram captions, a pinned-comment idea; long also chapters), `improvements.md` (what you could not judge, weaknesses, loudness). Then `python tools/produce.py done <id> finished/<kind>/<slug> <characters spent>`, `python tools/audit.py log` for authoring (note "wall time not measured" unless measured), update `finished/ideas.md`, and commit with the Co-Authored-By line from the session.

## Usage-friendly habits (the user is on a Claude Plus plan)
Read each reference file once; do not re-read files you just wrote. View images sparingly: one sheet per pass plus a handful of full-size stills (about 8 images for a Short, about 15 for a long). Reuse the template libraries and add only the new props the item needs. Write scripts and the shot builder in single Write calls. Run long renders in the background while you prepare the next item. If the user's usage limit warning appears, finish the current packet, mark progress with `produce.py done`, and stop cleanly.

## Stop conditions
1. The voice budget cannot cover the next item (say how many characters it needs and how many are left).
2. `tools/preflight`-style failures: missing voice id, ElevenLabs error (report the exact message), Chromium missing.
3. A topic cannot be verified and no reserve topic fits.
4. The continuity check or a review pass still fails after two fix rounds (report what is left).
Otherwise decide, do and report afterwards.

## Final report
For each item: title, the permanent path of `final.mp4` and of the thumbnail `.jpg` (1080x1920 for Shorts), duration, resolution, loudness, voice characters used (and the month total), claims with only `snippet` verification, anything you could not judge without hearing or watching (voice, motion, music), and the next item (`produce.py next`). Do not claim the audio sounds good. Never claim a video is live.
